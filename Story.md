## Prologue

1998.

It began as a breakthrough.

A virus engineered to redefine the future of medicine — the T-Virus — escaped containment. Within weeks, it stopped being an experiment and became a global collapse. Within months, it stopped being a crisis and became extinction.

Civilization did not fall overnight. It unraveled.

Cities went silent. Governments dissolved. Communication networks failed one by one, until the world was no longer connected — only scattered, decaying pockets of survival remained.

By 2003, the Umbrella Corporation no longer stood as a symbol of scientific progress. It stood as one of the last surviving remnants of a world that no longer existed. Its laboratories were abandoned or overrun, its supply chains broken, its influence reduced to isolated fragments of control.

The company that once shaped global biotechnology had become a survivor in its own collapse.

And survival was not enough.

---

## The Turning Point

For years, Umbrella operated in decline — maintaining fragments of infrastructure, preserving knowledge, and attempting to stabilize what little remained. But restoration was impossible within the existing world. The system itself had already failed.

There was only one conclusion left to reach:

To rebuild the corporation, the world itself would need to be rewritten.

---

## The Discovery

In September 2014, Dr. Vladimir Degtyarov’s research team achieved what was previously considered theoretical fiction — stable, controlled temporal displacement.

Time travel was no longer a hypothesis. It was an engineering reality.

For Umbrella’s Executive Board, the implication was immediate and absolute:

If the past could be reached, it could be changed.

If it could be changed, the collapse could be prevented.

And if the collapse never happened… Umbrella Corporation would never fall.

---

## The Directive

By November 2014, all remaining corporate assets were consolidated under a single directive: the Time Intervention Program.

Its objective was not recovery.

Not adaptation.

But correction.

A return to the moment before failure — before infection, before collapse, before extinction.

A single operation to restore what was lost by rewriting the origin of the catastrophe itself.

---

## Status

The mission is not without risk.

The timeline is not stable.

And the past does not accept intrusion without consequence.

But for Umbrella Corporation, there is no alternative future worth surviving in.
